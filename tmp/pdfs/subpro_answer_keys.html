41. c. refer to
“Refer to” is used because it means to consult a source of information or to look up information that one does not know. In this case, the question is asking what to do if one encounters a word whose meaning is not known. Rather than asking someone else for the definition, the best course of action is to refer to a dictionary or other reliable source of information to find the definition. Therefore, “refer to” is the most appropriate answer. The other options, such as “buy” or “borrow” a dictionary, or to “look inside” or “try” a dictionary, may be relevant in certain contexts, but they do not directly address the question of how to find the meaning of an unknown word.

 

42. a. identical to
“Identical to” is the correct preposition to use when comparing two things that are exactly the same. In this case, the sentence is comparing the speaker’s voice to another person’s voice and stating that they are very similar or almost indistinguishable.

 

The preposition “identical to” is commonly used when comparing nouns or pronouns that are exactly the same in terms of quality or quantity. In this sentence, the noun being compared is “your voice” and “hers.” Therefore, “identical to” is the correct preposition to use to indicate that the two voices are nearly identical.

 

The other options such as “same to” or “blends to” are not grammatically correct prepositions to use in this context, and “loud” or “different as” are not relevant to the comparison being made.

The phrase “keen on” is a common idiom used to express one’s strong interest in something. It is used to describe a person who has a genuine liking or enthusiasm for a particular activity or subject.

 

The other options such as “reliant on,” “intimidated by,” or “defective with” are not idiomatic expressions that would make sense in this context, and “awful with” is not relevant to the topic of interest or enthusiasm.

Instead of “told goodbye,” the correct expression is “said goodbye”. In English, we use the verb “say” or “bid” with the noun “goodbye” to indicate that we are leaving or departing from someone. 121. d. Php 3,900.00
Let the price of one table be t and the price of one chair be c. Then, we can create the following equations based on the given information:
10t = 4c (The price of 10 tables is equal to that of 4 chairs)
15t + 2c = 4000 (The price of 15 tables and 2 chairs together is Php 4,000)

 

From the first equation, we can simplify it to get:
t = 0.4c

 

Substituting this value of t into the second equation, we get:
15(0.4c) + 2c = 4000
6c + 2c = 4000
8c = 4000
c = 500

 

Substituting this value of c into the first equation, we get:
10t = 4(500)
t = 200

 

Therefore, the price of one table is Php 200 and the price of one chair is Php 500. Now, we can calculate the total price of 12 tables and 3 chairs as follows:
Total price = 12t + 3c
Total price = 12(200) + 3(500)
Total price = 2400 + 1500
Total price = Php 3900.00

123. a. 30 birds
Let’s assume that the probability of Kenny hitting a bird in one shot is p. Then, the probability of Max hitting a bird in one shot is 1/2, since he hits once in every two shots. Since Kenny fires 5 shots for every 3 shots fired by Max, the ratio of the number of shots fired by Kenny to the number of shots fired by Max is 5/3.

 

We can set up the following equation to relate the probabilities of Kenny hitting a bird and Max missing a bird:
5p = (2/3)(27)

 

Solving for p, we get:
p = (2/3)(27)/5
p = 18/25

 

Therefore, the probability of Kenny hitting a bird in one shot is 18/25. Now, we can calculate the total number of shots fired by Max as follows:
Let x be the number of birds killed by Max. Then, he has missed 27 times, which means he has fired a total of x + 27 shots.

 

Since Max kills one bird in every 2 shots, the number of birds killed by Max can be expressed as x = (1/2)(x + 27). Solving for x, we get:
x = 27

 

Therefore, Max killed a total of 27 birds. Using the ratio of shots fired by Kenny to shots fired by Max, we can calculate the number of shots fired by Kenny as follows:
Let y be the number of birds killed by Kenny. Then, he fires 5/3 times as many shots as Max, which means he has fired a total of (5/3)(x + 27) shots.

 

Since Kenny kills one bird in every 3 shots, the number of birds killed by Kenny can be expressed as y = (1/3)(5/3)(x + 27). Substituting x = 27, we get:
y = (1/3)(5/3) (27 + 27)
y = 30
Therefore, Kenny has killed a total of 30 birds. The answer is option (a) 30 birds.

125. c. 62.5 buckets
Let’s assume that each bucket has a capacity of “x” units of water, and the tank requires “y” units of water to be filled. Therefore, we can write:
25x = y (since 25 buckets are required to fill the tank)

 

Now, if the capacity of each bucket is reduced to two-fifth of its present size, the new capacity of each bucket would be (2/5)x units of water. Let’s say the number of buckets required to fill the tank with the new bucket size is “z”. Using the same logic as above, we can write:
z(2/5)x = y
(since z buckets are required to fill the tank with the new bucket size)

Therefore, the number of buckets required to fill the tank with the new bucket size is 62.5.

n(P or C) = n(P) + n(C) – n(P and C)
