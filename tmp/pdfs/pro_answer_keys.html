GRAMMAR and CORRECT USAGE:

Here are the answers to the Grammar and Correct Usage part of the CSE Professional Reviewer:

41. e. such as

The sentence is indicating that “professionals” is a category of people, and two specific examples of professions within that category are “lawyers” and “teachers”. The phrase “such as” is commonly used to introduce examples, and in this sentence, it is appropriate to use “such as” to indicate that lawyers and teachers are just two examples of the many professions that fall under the category of “professionals”.

 

42. d. who

In the sentence, “who” is being used to refer to the person who saw the speaker in person. This is because the sentence is describing a specific person with a particular role (a doctor), and “who” is used to introduce essential information about that person.

 

43. c. ought

In this sentence, “ought” is used as a modal auxiliary verb to express obligation or duty. It suggests that there is a moral or social expectation that something should be done about the suspicious transactions.

 

44. b. off

The phrasal verb “live off” means to depend on or survive on something, usually with financial connotations. In this sentence, the subject Marie is depending on juice as her primary source of sustenance for the purpose of losing weight.

 

45. b. appropriate

The adjective “appropriate” means suitable or fitting for a particular situation, and it is often used to describe behavior that is acceptable or proper in a given context. The rules should encourage behavior that is considered suitable or fitting in the workplace.

 

46. e. pride – stalked

A “pride” is the term used to describe a group of lions, and “to stalk” means to hunt stealthily or pursue quietly. Therefore, “pride – stalked” is the most appropriate choice to describe how the lions hunted the herd of deer in the sentence.

 

47. a. price – prize

The noun “price” refers to the cost of something, while “prize” refers to something that is awarded to a winner. In this sentence, “price” and “prize” are used to describe what made the winner jump for joy. It is possible that the winner won a valuable or rare prize that they had been hoping to acquire.

 

48. c. advised – advice

The verb “advise” means to give counsel or guidance, while the noun “advice” refers to the recommendations given. In this sentence, the IATF (Inter-Agency Task Force) is advising the local government units to seek the advice of adept medical specialists to address the worsening pandemic.

 

49. d. worth – worthy

“Worth” refers to the value or importance of something or someone, while “worthy” means deserving respect, attention, or admiration. The sentence is saying that it is not a good thing to try to prove your own value or importance by making other people feel like they are not deserving of respect or attention.

 

50. b. has started – seem

The correct answer is “has started – seem” because the sentence is referring to a present situation that began in the past and is still ongoing. “Has started” is in the present perfect tense, which is used to describe an action or event that began in the past and continues up to the present. Furthermore, the verb “seem” is in the present tense, which matches the tense of “has started.” Therefore, the correct answer is “has started – seem” to accurately convey that the pandemic’s impact is an ongoing situation and that health experts have found some reason to be optimistic despite the challenges.

 

51. a. You’ll need to turn left on Aguinaldo Street; it’s the third house on the right.

52. e. Since we have a game today, we have to leave campus early.

53. c. We had a campout and ate lots of foods, burgers, fries, pizza, chicken, and spaghetti.

This sentence uses proper punctuation to separate the list of foods with commas and a conjunction, to separate the list from the main clause.

54. d. The park had many attractions; it had a rollercoaster, a Ferris wheel, a merry-go-round, and a wild river ride.

This sentence uses a semicolon to separate the introductory clause from the main clause and commas to separate the items in the list of attractions, making it a clear and concise sentence.

55. a. Miss Dela Vega had gone to the United Kingdom to try to find a high-paying job.

This sentence uses the past perfect tense “had gone” to indicate that the action of going to the United Kingdom occurred before the time of the main clause, which is in the past tense (“to try to find”). The phrase “to try to find” is also correctly used to indicate the purpose of the action. Option b is also acceptable, using the simple past tense “went” and the phrase “to try and find” to indicate the purpose of the action. However, “try to find” is generally considered more correct than “try and find” in formal writing.

 

56. b. communications tools made to

The correct phrase should be “communication tools designed for.” “Made to” implies that the communications tools were created with the specific purpose of satisfying the demands of people who require more flexible communication services, which may not necessarily be the case. On the other hand, “designed for” implies that the communication tools were created with the intention of meeting the needs of individuals who require flexible communication services. This phrase accurately reflects the purpose of Facebook Messenger as a communication tool.

 

57. a. Here comes the handsome candidates

It should be “Here come the handsome candidates”. The correct verb form to use with “candidates” (plural) is “come,” not “comes.” “Come” is the third-person plural form of the verb “to come,” which matches the subject-verb agreement in this sentence.

58. a. With little patience

It should be “With a little patience”, which implies that the speaker believes that a small amount of patience is necessary to overcome difficult circumstances. Without the article “a,” the phrase “With little patience” may be interpreted as the speaker lacking patience, which is not the intended meaning of the sentence.
